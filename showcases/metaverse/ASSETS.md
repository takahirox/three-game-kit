## Authoring and preprocessing

`scripts/lib/metaverse-assets.mjs` (`lantern-court v1`) authors the assets with
Three.js and exports glTF 2.0 binary containers through GLTFExporter. It uses no
randomness. The visitor reuses the first-party Relic Ranger's 17-joint skeleton
and three locomotion clips from `scripts/lib/relic-ranger-rig.mjs`, replacing all
of that model's box artwork and weapon with original elliptical contour surfaces:
a tapered teal jacket, ochre scarf, rounded face and hair, eyes, terracotta trousers,
and boots. One vertex-coloured mesh is rigidly weighted per bone. The avatar faces
+Z, with feet at y=0 and a height of 2.02 metres. Clips animate in place; the public
Character Controller owns movement and the public Animation Feature blends clips.

The courtyard contains two open limestone arcades with curved extruded arches and
sweeping copper roof profiles, benches, six lathed ceramic planters with branching
stylised trees, and a lathed lantern. Ground, mosaic, paths and enclosing garden
walls are authored at runtime in the renderer. Simplified collider footprints in
`src/world.ts` match pillars, benches, planters, the lantern and perimeter.

Regenerate with `node scripts/build-metaverse-assets.mjs`. Run
`pnpm run test:metaverse-assets` to byte-compare deterministic regeneration, verify
model budgets, embedded buffers, skin and clips, landmark names, documentation,
and public import boundaries. No compression, external buffer or external texture
is used. The public Asset Manager loads both assets from local, base-relative URLs.

## Runtime ownership and visual verification

Asset Manager owns loaded model geometry/materials and disposes them after the
renderer and animation runtime release borrowed scene objects. The renderer owns
its ground/wall geometry, WebGL renderer, shadow target and generated skeleton bone
texture. Reset replaces the visitor's animation runtime and keeps the loaded assets;
disposal releases the entire runtime, host listeners and animation-frame callback.

The Playwright suite saves welcome and lit-lantern screenshots, checks actual bone
quaternion changes for idle and walk, named idle/walk/run states, model draw counts,
grounded placement, camera-relative facing, collision, and disposal. The rendered
courtyard and rounded visitor were visually reviewed during Issue #40 intake.
The gallery's two WebP covers are browser captures of this showcase, generated by
its deterministic public QA handle; no externally sourced images are included.
